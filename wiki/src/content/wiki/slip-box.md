This page summarizes the slip-box method, often called a Zettelkasten: one idea to a note, with pointers to the notes it depends on. It is a source page. The summary stays with that method and does not become a second essay about tools.

## The idea worth keeping

A slip is small enough to link. A new slip points at the older slips it uses. The value is not the cabinet. It is that a later reader, or a later model, can follow the pointers instead of rereading the whole pile.

An [[llm-wiki|LLM wiki]] asks a model to draft the slips a person used to write by hand. The discipline is the same: one claim, a link, and a date. The [[backlink|backlinks]] are the notes that point back.

Accounts of Niklas Luhmann's collection are mostly secondary, and this page does not quote them. The wording above is this wiki's own. The source below is a public overview of the method.
